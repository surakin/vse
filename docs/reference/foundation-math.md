# Foundation: Math

_Generated by docgen. Do not edit._

**Headers:** `engine/foundation/math/geometry.h`, `engine/foundation/math/math_util.h`, `engine/foundation/math/matrix.h`, `engine/foundation/math/placement.h`, `engine/foundation/math/quaternion.h`, `engine/foundation/math/transform.h`, `engine/foundation/math/vector.h`

Shapes and the tests between them: boxes, spheres, planes, rays and the view frustum, shared by the renderer's culling and the spatial module. Moved here from `engine/renderer/bounds.h` and `frustum.h`, which keep `using` aliases. Boxes and spheres are closed sets: shapes that only touch overlap, and a ray that grazes a face or an edge hits it.

Scalar math helpers: the constants; the functions (`Sqrt`, `Sin`, `Cos`, `Acos`, ...) are in `lang/scalar.h`, which this header includes.

Mat3 and Mat4: column-major storage with the column-vector convention.

Each matrix stores its columns directly. A vector transforms as `v' = M * v`, and composition is left-multiplication: `M2 * M1` applies `M1` first. This matches the GLM/OpenGL convention.

A placement in a world that is larger than single precision can hold: a rotation-and-scale part in `f32` and a translation in `f64`.

Quaternion rotations: Hamilton convention, right-handed, `w` is the scalar part.

Consistent with matrix.h: `ToMat3` rotates a vector the same way `RotateVector` does, and `a * b` rotates by `b` first, then by `a`.

Position, rotation and scale (TRS) and composition of two of them.

Uses the same column-vector convention as matrix.h and quaternion.h.

Vec2, Vec3 and Vec4: plain component structs with basic arithmetic, templates on the scalar type. `Vec2`, `Vec3` and `Vec4` are the single-precision forms (`Vec3T<f32>`); `Vec2d`, `Vec3d` and `Vec4d` are the double-precision forms (`Vec3T<f64>`), used for world positions.

A single-precision vector widens to a double-precision one implicitly (the conversion is exact); the other way is the named call `Narrow`, never an implicit conversion. No SIMD or special alignment. Index access uses an explicit switch rather than relying on struct layout.
