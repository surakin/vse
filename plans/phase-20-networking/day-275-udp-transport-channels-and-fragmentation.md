# Phase 20 / Day 275 — UDP Transport II: Channels, Reliability & Fragmentation

**Phase:** [Phase 20 — Networking](../phase-20-networking.md)
**Depends on:** Day 274.

The part games need and TCP does not give: per-message reliability choices and large messages over small packets.

## Tasks

- [x] **Channels:** unreliable, unreliable-sequenced (older messages dropped), reliable-ordered, reliable-unordered; each channel number independent so a stalled reliable-ordered channel never blocks another; per-channel send queues with caps and per-message priority within a packet budget.
- [x] **Reliable delivery:** resend from the ack state (not a per-message timer alone), resend timeout from the RTT estimate (Day 276 refines), a message acknowledged exactly once, a bounded window of unacknowledged data per connection, **ordered delivery with a bounded reorder buffer** (a peer that opens a gap beyond the buffer is disconnected, not buffered forever).
- [x] **Message packing:** several small messages share a packet up to the MTU; a message too large for one packet is fragmented (fragment id, count, index) and reassembled with a total-size limit declared up front, a per-connection reassembly memory budget, a timeout for incomplete messages, and rejection of overlapping or inconsistent fragments.

## Outcome (2026-11-12, Linux machine)

- Deviations from the task text: the random-message test sends 400 mixed-size messages over four channels (a million was not practical in a deterministic 1 ms-tick simulation; the conformance suite adds 600 per direction and the stress of every impairment); per-message priority within a packet is by channel number (a lower channel is sent first), not a separate value. The window is counted in frames (256), not bytes.
- Added: `Send` queues and `Poll` packs (several messages per packet); per-channel send queues; four reliability kinds with per-channel-and-kind sequence numbers; loss detection by acknowledged-packet distance (3) with an age condition, time-outs from the smoothed round trip, a 1024-entry sent-packet ring; ordered delivery with a reorder buffer (lookahead 1024, byte budget); fragmentation (1024-byte fragments, up to 65535) with exact validation, per-connection reassembly count and byte budgets, a time-out for unreliable partial messages; `UdpConnectionInfo::{frames_resent, buffered_bytes, reassemblies}`; `UdpTransportConfig::{max_message_bytes, max_queued_frames, window_frames, max_receive_buffer_bytes, max_reassemblies, reassembly_timeout_nanos}`.
- Found by the conformance suite and fixed: copies of fragments of completed messages leaked reassemblies until the limit ended the connection; a burst of 256 packets outran the 33-packet acknowledgement coverage (resend storm: 11,000 frames resent for 1,024, now about 1,200 at 5% loss with heavy reordering); a lost `Accepted` stranded one client of 40 at 60% loss (the server now repeats it on its own, and a client asks for a new cookie after one period).
- Tests: `tests/net/udp_channels_test.cpp` (13 conformance checks at 0, 5, 30 and 60% loss with reordering and duplication; a 1 MiB message intact under four impairments; 400 mixed messages once and in order; channel isolation; hand-built hostile fragments; duplicated fragments leave nothing behind; the message limit the wire can carry). The conformance suite waits longer on lossy carriers (`Lossy()`).
- Guide: `docs/guide/net.md` ("The UDP transport: channels, reliability and fragmentation").
- Runs: plain `ran 2757 of 7303 tests (phases: all; flavors: plain; display: included; crash tests: excluded)` (all passed), asan `ran 1935 of 6541 tests (phases: all; flavors: asan; display: included; crash tests: excluded)` (all passed), tsan `ran 1617 of 6541 tests (phases: all; flavors: tsan; display: included; crash tests: excluded)` (only the known tsan scene test failed); the UDP and simulated-network tests also pass on GCC, Clang non-unity and an optimized tree; `docgen lint` clean, docs 7 of 7.

## Post-implementation code review

`/code-review high`: 8 findings, 6 fixed, 2 declined.

- Fixed: `max_message_bytes` was clamped only inside the implementation, so `Send` and `MaxMessageBytes` used the unclamped value, and the 64 MiB clamp exceeded what a 16-bit fragment count holds (now 65535 x 1024 in one `ClampedConfig` used by both; test `a message limit the wire cannot carry is lowered`); duplicate fragments of completed messages leaked reassemblies (test `duplicated fragments of delivered messages leave nothing behind`, fails without the fix, checked); round-trip samples were taken from every packet an acknowledgement reported, including late ones (now only the newest); a lost `Accepted` could strand a client (server repeats it; client renews an old cookie); bursts longer than the acknowledgement coverage caused resend storms; a `(void)` cast and an odd initialiser in a test helper.
- Declined: an unreliable fragmented message is keyed by (channel, kind, sequence), so after the 16-bit sequence wraps inside the 5 s reassembly time-out a new message could complete a stale one (needs more than 13,000 unreliable fragmented messages per second on one channel; unreliable messages are rarely fragmented; filed in `KNOWN-ISSUES.md`); an unreliable frame waits behind a reliable one that the window holds back on the same channel (channels are the unit of ordering; use another channel for traffic that must not wait).
